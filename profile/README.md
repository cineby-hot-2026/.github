# Cineby

A catalogue of films and TV series at [cineby.hot](https://cineby.hot/).

This organisation is the GitHub home of the team that runs that catalogue. It is the same domain GitHub verified for us, and the same one linked from our Bluesky handle. The name Cineby has a history that is larger than this account, so this page records it: what the first Cineby was, how it ended, and where this project stands in relation to it.

## What happened, in one paragraph

A streaming site called Cineby appeared on the web in December 2024, grew into one of the largest unauthorized streaming operations, and shut down between August 26 and September 5, 2026 after blocking orders in the United Kingdom, Canada and India and pressure from the Motion Picture Association. Its servers were taken down and every user account was deleted. The domains that carried it are dead. This project started after that, in 2026, on its own domain and with its own catalogue.

## The first Cineby, 2024 to 2026

The earliest archived point is December 13, 2024, 13:26 UTC, on cineby.cc. A capture of cineby.app follows on December 26, 2024. Both addresses were later named in rights-holder submissions. Through 2025 and 2026 the operation moved between addresses rather than growing a single one: cineby.gd on October 25, 2025, cineby.sc on March 26, 2026, cineby.ws later that spring.

The move that matters is June 6, 2026. On that day cineby.cc began redirecting to cineby.at, and the first capture of the .at address is from the same day at 04:35 UTC. That pattern is a change of the main address, not a new mirror. Some later .cc captures still returned HTTP 200 through July 27, 2026, which the archive alone cannot explain: residual caching and incomplete redirects produce the same signature.

Full timeline, domain by domain: [cineby.hot/company/what-happened-to-cineby](https://cineby.hot/company/what-happened-to-cineby/)

## How large it got

Two measurements, two periods, two sources. They should not be averaged.

- August 2025, Similarweb figures cited by the MPA: 68.8 million monthly visits from 8.91 million unique visitors, world rank 789, with the United States supplying the largest share of traffic.
- July 2026, reported by TorrentFreak: more than 160 million visits in the month.

Using 160 million as the comparison point, the later figure is roughly 2.33 times the earlier estimate. That comparison is directional only: different months, different measurement periods, visits rather than unique viewers.

On August 25, 2026, two companion sites, Fmovies+ and Cineplay, began redirecting their visitors to Cineby. The announcement of closure followed a day later.

## What the service actually ran on

Cineby did not keep a library of video files. It embedded streams supplied by third parties, and the MPA identified two of those suppliers: Videasy for video on demand and Streamed for live sports. This is the piracy-as-a-service arrangement: the front end, the catalogue and the player interface are one company, the streams are another.

The model is cheap to start and fragile to run. In its 2026 submission the MPA described Videasy as offline and Vidking as reachable but without connected source servers. At that point a working catalogue and a working player were no longer enough to deliver a stream.

## Legal pressure

- Fall 2025: the MPA named cineby.app in its submission to the U.S. Trade Representative for the Notorious Markets review, describing it as a rapidly growing piracy portal.
- May 2026: a UK omnibus blocking order covered Cineby and, explicitly, future successors. A Canadian order of expanded scope did the same. Both were written to survive a change of domain.
- July 2026: a Delhi High Court injunction was reported to cover four Cineby domains, directing Indian internet providers to block them and registrars to suspend them. The figure of 248 domains that circulates in coverage belongs to a different, earlier Delhi action, not to this one.
- October 2026: the MPA attributed the shutdown of Cineby-associated domains and of Videasy to MPA and ACE actions. Its filing does not name one court ruling, seizure or police operation as the trigger.

## The shutdown

The closure was announced on August 26, 2026 and staged over ten days. Servers were switched off in sequence. The operators set September 5, 2026 as the final date, and by then the remaining addresses stopped serving the catalogue. Every user account was deleted with the servers. No export, no migration, no successor named by the operators.

## What is left

Addresses that once served Cineby now return errors, redirects or parking pages, and several same-named sites appeared to collect the audience that was left behind. Rights-holder documents describe at least one of those copies as a copycat. A name that is reused does not establish continuity, and the original team did not endorse anyone.

Practical consequence for a visitor: if a site claims to be the old Cineby and asks for an account, a card or an install, it is not operating on anything the original team left behind. Nothing was left behind.

## This catalogue, built in 2026

The project behind this account is separate. It was registered in Hanoi, Vietnam, in 2025 and rebuilt a catalogue from scratch in 2026, with a first archive capture of cineby.hot on November 3, 2026. It is run by a team of three.

What it does: films and series with cast, running time, seasons and episodes, and ratings shown together with the source they came from. Browsing by genre, year and country, with filters that combine. A watchlist, folders, ratings and viewing history for anyone who signs in with a one-time email link or with Google.

What it does not do: it holds no accounts from the previous service, because those accounts were deleted in September 2026. It carries no catalogue from that service. It does not claim to be the continuation of a company or a brand.

## Why the name is the same

Because the name describes the thing: a catalogue of cinema. The earlier service is gone, and this one starts from an empty database rather than from an inheritance. Anyone comparing the two will find different catalogues, different accounts and a different team, which is the honest way to settle the question.
